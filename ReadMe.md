# Motivation

When the Allied Powers obtained Japan's surrender, one of the main objectives of the ensuing presence in Japan was to make sure that it developed into a democracy rather than remaining an erstwhile monarchy. One of the ways they adopted to do so was to change the patriarchal norms in society through radio messaging.

Okuyama studies how radio broadcasting brought about changes in economic and political variables relating to women.

# Identification Strategy

The main hypothesis is the effect of radio listenership on various outcomes. But here lies the problem: we might think it is radio listenership which increased women's turnout in elections.

However, what if radio subscription rates were higher in towns where civic awareness might already have been on the rise, affecting both radio subscription rates and voter turnout? Or the towns may simply have had more educated citizens who might subscribe to radio in larger numbers and turn out for voting more.

To counter this problem, Okuyama uses radio signal strength as an IV.

Radio signal strength fulfills both conditions for an ideal IV:

a) Radio signal strength affects radio subscription rates, which is true because you are less likely to subscribe to radio if the signal strength itself is weak.

b) Signal strength only affects economic and demographic variables through radio subscription. For example, if radio signal strength is stronger near urban regions, and being in an urban region also leads to higher voter turnout, then there is no use in having such an IV. After controlling for these factors (log no. HH), our IV passes the test.

# Results

- Women's turnout increased in elections.
- Labor force participation remained unchanged.
- Employment rates remained unchanged.

Is this a failure of the main thesis? No. Okuyama argues that increased awareness among women does not necessarily translate into a change in the attitudes of men who hire women.

# Replication

All the results matched to the last two decimal places.
